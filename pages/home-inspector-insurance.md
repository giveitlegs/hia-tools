# Home Inspector Insurance Cost Estimator

Errors and omissions (E&O) insurance for a home inspector typically costs **$600 to $2,500 a year**, and general liability adds about **$400 to $1,000**. Your premium depends on inspection volume, limits, deductible, ancillary services and claims history. 32 states set a minimum insurance or bond requirement for licensed inspectors.

## How to use the estimator

1. Pick your state to see its insurance rule.
2. Enter inspections per year, your E&O limits and deductible.
3. Tick the ancillary services you offer, such as mold, radon or termite (WDO).
4. Add years in business and claims history, then open "Assumptions" to see how each choice moved the estimate.

## What the two main policies cover

- **Errors and omissions (professional liability)** pays for your defense and settlements when a client says you missed a defect or reported something wrong. Most claims against inspectors fall under this policy.
- **General liability** covers bodily injury and property damage during an inspection, such as a cracked tile or a ceiling damaged in the attic.
- Many brokers sell the two as a bundle. Some states accept a bond in place of insurance.

## Methodology

The estimator starts from a typical E&O premium of $1,000 a year at $1M/$1M limits (the Insureon median is $989). It adjusts by $50 to $300 per limit tier, adds 1% to 15% per ancillary endorsement and takes 3% to 15% off per higher deductible tier, all as published by InspectorPro. High volume, recent claims or a new business move the estimate toward the top of the range. General liability uses the published range. It is an estimate, not a quote. Dataset: [insurance.json](https://github.com/giveitlegs/hia-tools/blob/main/data/insurance.json), checked 2026-11-21.

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### How much is home inspector insurance?
Published figures put E&O at about $600 to $2,500 a year and general liability at about $400 to $1,000. Insureon reports a median of $82 a month for E&O and $45 a month for general liability.
:::

::: faq
### Do home inspectors need E&O insurance?
Some states require it as a condition of the license, including New Jersey ($500,000 per occurrence) and Rhode Island ($500,000 aggregate). In other states it is optional but widely carried, because most claims against inspectors are professional liability claims.
:::

::: faq
### What limits should a home inspector carry?
Brokers commonly offer $100,000 up to $1M per claim, and InspectorPro says $1M/$1M is its most popular choice. Your state minimum is the floor.
:::

::: faq
### Does offering mold or radon testing raise my premium?
Usually. InspectorPro reports that endorsements for services such as mold or pool and spa each add about 1% to 15% to the premium.
:::

::: faq
### How can I lower my premium?
Choose a higher deductible, keep a clean claims history, use a strong pre-inspection agreement, and keep up with continuing education. Some programs give discounts for experience and loss-free years.
:::

::: faq
### Is general liability required for home inspectors?
Several states require it, often $100,000 to $500,000. Florida, for example, requires $300,000 of commercial general liability. See the state table above.
:::
