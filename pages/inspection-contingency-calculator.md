# Inspection Contingency Deadline Calculator

Most home inspection contingency periods run **7 to 17 days** from the date the contract is accepted, and 10 days is the most common. Some states' standard forms set a default (California 17 days, Florida 15, Illinois 5 business days); others leave it to negotiation. Enter your acceptance date and contract terms below to get every deadline and add them to your calendar.

## How to use the calculator

1. Choose your state's contract, if it is listed. The default period, day type and seller response period fill in automatically.
2. Enter the contract acceptance (effective) date.
3. Change the number of days to match your signed contract.
4. Choose calendar or business days. Business days skip weekends and US federal holidays.
5. Download the .ics file to add the dates to Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar.

## A typical inspection timeline

1. **Days 1–2:** book the home inspector and any add-ons such as [radon](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/radon-testing/), [sewer scope](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/sewer-scope-inspection/) or [termite](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/termite-inspection/).
2. **First third of the period:** the inspection happens. Radon tests need at least 48 hours.
3. **Middle:** read the report, call specialists, and get contractor quotes for big items.
4. **Before the deadline:** send your repair request, credit request or termination notice in writing.
5. **After the deadline:** the seller responds, and you accept, counter or walk away under your contract's terms.

## Methodology

The calculator counts from the day after acceptance. In business-day mode it skips Saturdays, Sundays and US federal holidays (observed dates). Suggested dates for booking and finishing inspections are planning aids, not contract terms. Form defaults come from each state association or regulator, checked 2026-11-21. Dataset: [contingency.json](https://github.com/giveitlegs/hia-tools/blob/main/data/contingency.json). This is not legal advice. Talk to your agent or attorney.

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### How many days do you have after a home inspection?
It depends on your contract. Most inspection contingencies run 7 to 17 days from acceptance, and the deadline covers the inspection, your review and your repair request.
:::

::: faq
### What is a standard home inspection contingency period?
National sources most often cite 7 to 10 days. California's form defaults to 17 days, Florida's AS IS contract to 15 days, and the Chicago-area Multi-Board 8.0 contract to 5 business days.
:::

::: faq
### Does the contingency period include weekends?
If your contract counts calendar days, yes. If it counts business days, weekends and legal holidays are skipped. Some forms also move a deadline that lands on a weekend to the next business day.
:::

::: faq
### Can you extend the home inspection period?
Only by written agreement with the seller, usually an amendment to the contract. Ask early if a specialist cannot come in time.
:::

::: faq
### What happens if I miss the inspection deadline?
You may lose the right to cancel or to request repairs under the contingency, and your earnest money may be at risk. Read your contract and talk to your agent right away.
:::

::: faq
### Is the Texas option period the same as an inspection contingency?
It works differently. The buyer pays an option fee for an unrestricted right to terminate during a negotiated number of days. There is no default length.
:::
