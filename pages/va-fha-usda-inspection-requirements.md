# VA, FHA & USDA Inspection Requirements

FHA, VA and USDA loans all require the home to meet **minimum property requirements**: it must be safe, structurally sound and sanitary. The appraiser checks these on site, and items like a leaking roof, peeling paint on an older home, missing handrails or a broken heating system must be fixed before closing. The appraisal is not a home inspection. Choose your loan type below to see the rules that apply to your property.

## How to use the checklist

1. Choose FHA, VA or USDA.
2. Set the property type, water source and sewage system. Well, septic, condo and manufactured-home rules appear only when they apply.
3. For a VA loan, choose your state to see whether a termite (wood-destroying insect) report is required.
4. Review the items marked **Common repair** first, then print the list for your agent or seller.

## The three programs at a glance

- **FHA:** FHA's Minimum Property Requirements say every insured home must be safe, sound and secure. The FHA appraiser checks this on site as the lender's representative and conditions the appraisal on any required repairs (II.A.3.a.ii; appraiser standards in II.D.3). These are the rules in effect in Handbook 4000.1 Update 18 (published 8/12/2026). HUD posted a proposed MPR rewrite for comment in September 2026, but it was not final as of 2026-11-21.
- **VA:** VA's Minimum Property Requirements make sure a home is safe, structurally sound and sanitary before VA guarantees the loan (Ch.12 Topic 1). VA fee appraisers recommend repairs, not inspections. This version applies to appraisals ordered after May 1, 2026 (Change 46, 2/27/2026), which removed the non-vented heater, detached-improvement and radon provisions and renumbered Topics 34-43.
- **USDA:** USDA requires an existing dwelling to meet the current minimum property requirements of HUD Handbook 4000.1, and a qualified appraiser may certify that it does (HB-1-3555 12.9(A)). USDA adds its own rules for well water testing and septic evaluations (12.6), environmental hazards and flood zones (12.10), and manufactured homes (Ch.13). New construction must instead meet ICC/IECC codes and USDA's inspection and warranty rules (12.9(B)).

## Termite (WDO) reports

- **FHA:** For existing homes, a termite or wood-destroying insect inspection is required only when the appraiser sees evidence of infestation or prior treatment, or is notified of one. The appraisal is then made subject to inspection by a qualified pest control specialist, and the lender must get the inspection plus proof of any treatment before approving the property (HUD 4000.1 II.D.3.m.iii; II.A.3.a.ii(Q)). Soil poisoning is an unacceptable treatment unless the water supply is protected (II.A.3.a.ii(Q)). For new construction, a builder's termite guarantee (form HUD-NPMA-99-A, plus NPMA-99-B if treated) is required unless the county is on HUD's Termite Treatment Exception Areas list (II.A.8.i.iv).
- **VA:** A wood-destroying insect report is required (1) whenever infestation or damage is apparent, with all damage repaired (M26-7 Ch.12 Topic 33a), and (2) on every origination appraisal in areas rated 'very heavy' or 'moderate to heavy' on the Termite Infestation Probability Map (Topic 33b). VA's Local Requirements page lists the affected states (last updated 7/11/2025): entire-state requirement in AL, AZ, AR, CA, CT, DE, DC, FL, GA, HI, IL, IN, KS, KY, LA, MD, MA, MS, MO, NJ, NM, NC, OH, OK, RI, SC, TN, TX, VA, WV and the territories; certain counties only in CO, IA, NE, NV, NY, PA, UT and WI. In unlisted states, no report is needed unless the appraisal notes a problem. High-rise condo units are exempt, and side-by-side condo or townhome units in required zones need a report unless the HOA shows evidence of treatment (Topic 33e).
- **USDA:** A termite or pest inspection is required when the lender, appraiser, inspector or state law requires one to confirm the property is free of active infestation (HB-1-3555 12.9(A)). Because USDA adopts HUD 4000.1 for existing dwellings, FHA's rule also applies: evidence of infestation means inspection and treatment.

Need one? Find a [termite inspector](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/termite-inspection/).

## Wells and septic systems

- **FHA:** Well water must meet local or state health standards, or EPA standards (40 CFR 141-142) where there are none. A well water test is required for new construction, when the appraiser reports a well or water deficiency, when the water is known to be unsafe, or when the home is near dumps, landfills, industrial sites or farms that use pesticides (HUD 4000.1 II.A.3.a.ii(N)(2)(a)). The appraiser must also require a test or inspection for nearby intensive agriculture, mining or drilling, or a dump, gas station or dry cleaner within a quarter mile, among other triggers (II.D.3.o.v(C)). A disinterested third party must collect and test the sample, and the report may be no more than 180 days old at disbursement (II.A.3.a.ii(N)(2)(b)). Minimum distances for existing construction: 10 ft from the property line, 50 ft from the septic tank and 100 ft from the drain field (75 ft if local authority allows); greater local distances prevail. Existing wells must deliver at least 3 gpm, and new ones 5 gpm over four hours (II.A.3.a.ii(N)(2)(a); 24 CFR 200.926d(f)). Wells need a pressure tank of at least 42 gallons or equivalent (II.D.3.o.v(C)). Septic systems must dispose of waste without nuisance or health hazard, and an inspection is required if there are signs of failure. A failed system must be connected to available public sewer or repaired or replaced to health department standards (II.D.3.p; II.A.3.a.ii(P)).
- **VA:** Well water must meet the local health authority's requirements, or EPA guidelines if there are none, and a disinterested third party must collect and test the sample (M26-7 Ch.12 Topic 16a-b). Results are valid for 90 days unless the local authority says otherwise (Topic 16f). The appraiser must know local well-to-pollution-source distances but is not required to measure them, and Chapter 12 states no numeric setbacks (Topic 16e). Dug wells, cisterns, hauled water, chlorinators, springs, lakes, rivers, sand-point or artesian wells and rainwater catchment require the Veteran's written acknowledgment (Topic 16c). A septic system must dispose of all waste in a sanitary manner, and health authority approval is required for proposed construction, when the appraiser notes a problem, or where soils have known percolation problems (Topic 17a, 17c). Connection to public water or sewer is required where the local authority mandates it (Topic 15g).
- **USDA:** Every individual private well needs a water quality test by the local health authority or a state-certified lab, no more than 180 days old at closing, meeting state or local standards or EPA maximum contaminant levels (HB-1-3555 12.6(A)(1)). The distance between the well and the septic system must be measured and must meet HUD 4000.1 or be acceptable to the local or state health authority (12.6(A)(1); 12.6(B)(1)). The lender must get a septic evaluation, which may come from a qualified appraiser certifying the home meets HUD 4000.1, a health authority, a licensed septic professional or a qualified home inspector. The system must show no evidence of failure and must sit entirely on the property, or on recorded easements (12.6(B)(1)). In flood zones, homes on septic need a water supply protected from cross-contamination (12.10(B)).

See [well water testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/well-water-testing/) and [septic inspections](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/septic-inspection/).

## Appraisal vs. home inspection

- HUD form HUD-92564-CN, 'For Your Protection: Get a Home Inspection' (rev. 02/09/2023), states that an appraisal estimates the home's value for the lender and does not replace a home inspection. FHA does not guarantee the value or condition of the home and cannot lend money for repairs or buy the home back if problems surface after closing.
- FHA does not perform home inspections; one only happens if the buyer arranges and pays for it (HUD-92564-CN). FHA lenders must give the form to prospective buyers at first contact (HUD 4000.1 II.A.1.a.ii(A)(3)), and USDA lenders must also provide it and keep evidence in the loan file (HB-1-3555 12.9(A)).
- VA's Lenders Handbook says an appraisal is not a home inspection: the fee appraiser does not perform operational checks of mechanical systems or appliances and estimates value to make sure it supports the loan (M26-7 Ch.12 Topic 1b). The Notice of Value issued to the Veteran recommends getting a home inspection (Topic 1g).
- Appraisers observe only readily accessible, readily observable conditions. For example, FHA appraisers are not required to disturb insulation, move items or put tools inside the electrical panel (HUD 4000.1 II.D.3.f.ii, II.D.3.j-l), VA appraisers do not have to climb onto roofs or into attics (M26-7 Ch.12 Topics 26c, 27a), and an FHA appraiser's pest observation 'is not required to be at the same level as a qualified pest control specialist' (II.D.3.m.iii).
- USDA requires lenders to encourage buyers to get a detailed home inspection independent of the appraisal (HB-1-3555 12.9(A)), and HUD's form recommends radon testing and asking the inspector about mold, water quality, asbestos, lead paint and pests (HUD-92564-CN).

A home inspector looks much more closely than an appraiser. For an FHA, VA or USDA purchase, book both. Find [inspectors for FHA, VA and USDA loans](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/fha-va-usda-home-inspection/).

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### Does an FHA loan require a home inspection?
No. FHA requires an appraisal that checks minimum property requirements, but not a home inspection. HUD's form 'For Your Protection: Get a Home Inspection' strongly recommends one.
:::

::: faq
### Does a VA loan require a home inspection?
No. The VA appraisal checks minimum property requirements. VA recommends a separate home inspection, and a termite report is required in many states.
:::

::: faq
### What fails an FHA or VA appraisal?
Common items are roof leaks or a worn-out roof, peeling paint on homes built before 1978, missing handrails, broken windows, inoperable heating, exposed wiring, water in the crawlspace, and signs of termites.
:::

::: faq
### Who pays for FHA or VA required repairs?
It is negotiated. Sellers often make the repairs so the loan can close, but buyers can agree to pay or the price can be adjusted. The repairs usually must be finished before closing.
:::

::: faq
### Does USDA follow FHA property standards?
Yes, for existing homes USDA uses HUD Handbook 4000.1 minimum property requirements, with added rules for wells, septic systems and manufactured homes.
:::

::: faq
### Is a well water test required for an FHA loan?
When the appraiser notes a well problem, for new construction, or when the area has known contamination risks. The test must be done by a disinterested third party.
:::

::: faq
### Do VA loans require a termite inspection?
In states rated moderate to very heavy for termites, yes, on every origination appraisal. Use the state selector in the tool to check yours.
:::
