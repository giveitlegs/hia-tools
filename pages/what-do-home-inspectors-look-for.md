# What Do Home Inspectors Look For?

A home inspector looks for material defects in the roof, structure, electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling systems, attic, exterior, interior and garage: problems that could affect the home's value or the safety of the people living in it. ## What inspectors check in each part of the house

### Roof (SOP 3.1)

**What the inspector checks:** Roof covering (shingles, tile, metal, flat membrane), viewed from the roof, the eaves or the ground; Gutters and downspouts; Flashing, vents, skylights, chimney and other penetrations; Roof structure from accessible attic areas.

**Common defects:** Missing, curled, cracked or granule-worn shingles; Lifted or rusted flashing at walls and chimneys; Active leaks or stains below the roof; Gutters pulled loose or draining against the foundation.

**Usually not included:** Walking an unsafe or fragile roof; Predicting how many years the roof has left; Water testing or certifying the roof; Underground downspout drains.

**Related:** [Roof inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/roof-inspection/), [4-point inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/4-point-inspection/), [Wind mitigation inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/wind-mitigation-inspection/)

### Attic (SOP 3.9)

**What the inspector checks:** Insulation type and approximate depth; Ventilation of the attic space; Bath, kitchen and laundry exhaust fans and where they vent; Visible roof framing and sheathing.

**Common defects:** Thin or missing insulation; Fans venting into the attic instead of outside; Staining or mold-like growth on sheathing from poor ventilation; Signs of rodents or pests.

**Usually not included:** Entering an attic that is not safely accessible; Moving or disturbing insulation; Determining R-value or whether ventilation is adequate.

**Related:** [Thermal imaging](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/thermal-imaging-inspection/), [Mold inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/mold-inspection/), [Air quality testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/air-quality-testing/)

### Exterior (SOP 3.2)

**What the inspector checks:** Siding, trim, eaves, soffits and fascia; Exterior doors and a representative number of windows; Decks, porches, balconies, stairs, railings and guards; Grading, drainage, walkways, driveways and retaining walls where they affect the house.

**Common defects:** Wood rot at trim and door frames; Loose deck ledgers, wobbly railings, unsafe baluster spacing; Negative grading sending water toward the foundation; Cracked stucco or siding gaps letting water in.

**Usually not included:** Fences, outbuildings and exterior lighting; Pools, spas and playground equipment; Septic systems, wells and irrigation; Soil, geological or flood conditions.

**Related:** [Stucco inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/stucco-inspection/), [Pool inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/pool-inspection/), [Lead paint inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/lead-paint-inspection/)

### Foundation and crawlspace (SOP 3.3)

**What the inspector checks:** Foundation type and visible condition; Basement and crawlspace, where accessible; Visible structural framing: joists, beams, posts; Signs of water entry and movement.

**Common defects:** Cracks, bowing walls or displacement; Water staining, efflorescence or standing water; Wood touching soil, rot or insect damage; Cut or notched joists.

**Usually not included:** Engineering judgements about structural adequacy; Entering a crawlspace that is unsafe or too tight; Moving stored items to see walls and floors.

**Related:** [Foundation inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/foundation-inspection/), [Termite inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/termite-inspection/), [Radon testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/radon-testing/)

### Electrical (SOP 3.7)

**What the inspector checks:** Service drop, meter, service-entrance conductors and main disconnect; Panelboards and breakers or fuses; Grounding and bonding; A representative number of outlets, switches and fixtures; GFCI and AFCI devices; Presence of smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.

**Common defects:** Double-tapped breakers and open panel slots; Missing GFCI protection near water; Reversed polarity or ungrounded outlets; Solid-conductor aluminum branch wiring; Missing smoke or CO alarms.

**Usually not included:** Low-voltage wiring, security, phone and data systems; Generators and solar panels; Testing smoke alarms or resetting breakers; Voltage-drop calculations.

**Related:** [Electrical inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/electrical-inspection/), [Thermal imaging](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/thermal-imaging-inspection/)

### Plumbing (SOP 3.6)

**What the inspector checks:** Main water and fuel shut-off valves; Water heater, venting and TPR valve; Supply flow at fixtures, toilets, drains and visible drain-waste-vent piping; Sump pumps with accessible floats.

**Common defects:** Active leaks under sinks and at toilets; Low flow with two fixtures running; Missing or improper TPR discharge pipe; Slow drains and loose toilets.

**Usually not included:** Water quality or well output; Septic systems and sewer lines under the yard; Water softeners and filters; Operating shut-off valves.

**Related:** [Plumbing inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/plumbing-inspection/), [Sewer scope inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/sewer-scope-inspection/), [Well water testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/well-water-testing/), [Septic inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/septic-inspection/)

### Heating and cooling (SOP 3.4 / 3.5)

**What the inspector checks:** Heating system operation from the thermostat; Cooling system operation from the thermostat; Energy source, method and thermostat location; Visible venting, filters and ductwork.

**Common defects:** System does not respond to the thermostat; Rusted or improperly vented gas appliances; Dirty filters and condensate leaks; Equipment near or past typical service life (noted, not a defect on its own).

**Usually not included:** Heat exchanger interiors; Sizing, BTU capacity and airflow balance; Running AC below 65°F outdoor temperature; Window and through-wall units.

**Related:** [HVAC inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/hvac-inspection/), [Air quality testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/air-quality-testing/), [Chimney inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/chimney-inspection/)

### Interior rooms (SOP 3.8 / 3.10)

**What the inspector checks:** Floors, walls and ceilings; A representative number of doors and windows; Stairs, railings and guards; Fireplace: visible firebox, damper, hearth and lintel.

**Common defects:** Ceiling stains from roof or plumbing leaks; Fogged double-pane windows (failed seals); Loose handrails and wide baluster gaps; Missing smoke or CO alarm in a room with a fireplace.

**Usually not included:** Paint, wallpaper and floor coverings; Appliances not permanently installed; Moving furniture or rugs; Chimney flue interiors.

**Related:** [Mold inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/mold-inspection/), [Asbestos testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/asbestos-testing/), [Chimney inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/chimney-inspection/)

### Garage (SOP 3.10)

**What the inspector checks:** Garage vehicle doors and openers; Auto-reverse and photo-eye safety sensors; Fire separation between the garage and the house; Slab, walls and visible framing.

**Common defects:** Opener that does not reverse or sensors out of alignment; Non-fire-rated door into the house; Holes in the fire-separation wall; Slab cracks with water entry.

**Usually not included:** Equipment stored in the garage; Certifying auto-reverse safety; Detached outbuildings (often excluded unless agreed).

**Related:** [Pre-purchase inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/pre-purchase-home-inspection/), [New construction inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/new-construction-inspection/)

## What a home inspection is not

A home inspection is a visual, non-invasive look at readily accessible areas on one day. It is not a code inspection, an appraisal, an engineering report or a warranty. Inspectors do not predict how long a roof or furnace will last, open walls or move furniture. Environmental tests such as [radon](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/radon-testing/), [mold](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/mold-inspection/), [asbestos](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/asbestos-testing/) and [lead paint](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/lead-paint-inspection/) are separate services.

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### What do home inspectors look for?
Material defects in the roof, exterior, foundation and structure, electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling, attic and insulation, interior, fireplace and garage. They also note safety hazards such as missing GFCI protection or smoke alarms.
:::

::: faq
### What are the most common problems found in a home inspection?
Roof wear and flashing leaks, poor grading and drainage, electrical panel issues, missing GFCI outlets, plumbing leaks, aging water heaters and HVAC equipment, and attic ventilation problems.
:::

::: faq
### Do home inspectors check for mold?
Not as part of a standard inspection. They report visible moisture and conditions that support mold growth. Mold sampling is a separate service.
:::

::: faq
### Do home inspectors go on the roof?
Often, when it is safe. The standards let inspectors view the roof from the ground, the eaves or by drone instead of walking a steep, wet or fragile roof.
:::

::: faq
### What does a home inspector not check?
Inside walls, sewer lines under the yard, septic systems, wells, pools, radon, code compliance, and items not permanently installed, unless you book those services separately.
:::

::: faq
### What does a VA or FHA appraisal look for compared with an inspection?
The appraiser checks minimum property requirements for the loan, such as a sound roof and safe utilities. It is not a full inspection. See our FHA, VA and USDA requirements checklist.
:::
