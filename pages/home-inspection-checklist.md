# Home Inspection Checklist

Use this free checklist to follow along during a home inspection, room by room. Switch between buyer, seller pre-listing, new construction, condo and manufactured home versions, tick items as you go, flag anything you want to ask the inspector about, and print or save the list as a PDF. Your progress stays in your browser.

## How to use the checklist

1. Choose your checklist type: buyer, seller pre-listing, new construction, condo or manufactured home.
2. Bring it to the inspection on your phone, or print it first.
3. Tick each item once you have seen it or the inspector has covered it.
4. Tap **Flag** on anything that worries you and add a short note, for example "stain on bedroom 2 ceiling".
5. At the end, review the "Ask your inspector about these" list before the inspector leaves.

## The buyer's checklist

The items follow the InterNACHI Standards of Practice, the scope most inspectors work to. Section numbers are in brackets.

### Before the inspection

- Book the inspection inside your contingency window and note the deadline
- Confirm the inspector's state license (where required) and insurance
- Add-ons booked if needed: radon, sewer scope, termite/WDO, mold, septic, well water

### Exterior and grounds (SOP 3.2)

- Grading slopes away from the foundation; no standing water near walls
- Gutters and downspouts attached and discharging away from the house
- Siding, trim, eaves, soffits and fascia: rot, gaps, missing pieces, peeling paint
- Exterior doors close, latch and seal; weatherstripping intact
- Decks, porches, balconies: ledger attachment, loose boards, guard height and baluster spacing
- Walkways, driveway and steps: trip hazards, settlement, handrails where needed
- Vegetation and trees clear of the roof and siding
- Retaining walls: leaning, cracking, missing drainage

### Roof (SOP 3.1)

- Roof covering type noted; missing, curled or cracked shingles or tiles
- Flashing at chimneys, walls, skylights and vents
- Signs of active leaks at ceilings and in the attic
- Roof age and remaining life asked of the seller (affects insurance in some states)
- Chimney exterior: cracked crown, loose bricks, missing cap

### Structure, foundation and crawlspace (SOP 3.3)

- Foundation type noted; cracks, bowing walls or displacement
- Signs of movement: sticking doors, out-of-square frames, sloping floors, drywall cracks
- Water in the basement or crawlspace; efflorescence, staining, sump pump condition
- Wood in contact with soil; rot or wood-destroying insect damage
- Cut, notched or bored joists and beams
- Crawlspace vapor barrier, insulation and ventilation

### Attic, insulation and ventilation (SOP 3.9)

- Insulation type and approximate depth
- Attic ventilation; staining, mold-like growth or frost on roof sheathing
- Bath and kitchen fans vent outside, not into the attic
- Evidence of pests or rodents in the attic

### Electrical (SOP 3.7)

- Service size (amps) and panel type noted; known problem panels flagged
- Open panel slots, double-tapped breakers, scorch marks
- GFCI protection in kitchen, baths, garage, exterior and near water
- Representative outlets tested for power, polarity and grounding
- Solid-conductor aluminum branch wiring or knob-and-tube (older homes)
- Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms present
- Service drop clearance and condition of the meter and mast

### Plumbing (SOP 3.6)

- Main water and fuel shut-offs located
- Water pressure and flow with two fixtures running
- Leaks under sinks and around toilets; loose toilets
- Drainage speed at sinks, tubs and showers
- Water heater age, TPR valve and discharge pipe, venting
- Supply pipe material noted (copper, PEX, galvanized, polybutylene)
- Sewer scope booked for older homes or homes with mature trees
- Private well and septic: separate testing and pumping arranged

### Heating and cooling (SOP 3.4 / 3.5)

- Heating system runs from the thermostat; energy source and method noted
- Cooling system runs (inspectors may skip it below 65°F)
- Equipment age and service records requested
- Filters, visible ductwork and condensate drain
- Flue and venting connections for gas appliances

### Interior rooms (SOP 3.10)

- Representative doors and windows open, close and latch
- Fogged double-pane windows (failed seals)
- Ceilings and walls: water stains, cracks, soft spots
- Stairs and railings: loose handrails, baluster spacing, guard height
- Floors: soft spots, sloping, damaged subfloor near wet areas

### Kitchen and bathrooms (SOP 3.6 / 3.10)

- Sink, faucet and drain operation; leaks in the base cabinet
- Tub and shower surrounds: cracked tile, failed caulk, soft walls
- Exhaust fans work and are ducted outside
- Signs of moisture or mold-like growth around fixtures
- Appliances: ask which convey; most inspectors only test built-ins at their discretion

### Fireplace (SOP 3.8)

- Damper opens and closes; firebox and hearth intact
- Smoke and CO alarms in the same room as the fireplace
- Level 2 chimney inspection booked if you plan to use it

### Garage (SOP 3.10)

- Garage door opener auto-reverse and photo-eye sensors
- Fire-rated door and wall between the garage and the house
- Slab cracks and signs of water entry

### After the inspection

- Read the summary page first; sort items into safety, major, and maintenance
- Get contractor quotes for big-ticket items before your deadline
- Send a repair request or credit request using your state's addendum form

## Seller, new construction, condo and manufactured home versions

- **Seller pre-listing:** adds the prep work that speeds up a buyer's inspection: utilities on, clear access to the panel, attic and crawlspace, a new furnace filter and working alarms. See [pre-listing home inspections](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/pre-listing-home-inspection/).
- **New construction:** adds the pre-pour, pre-drywall, final and 11-month warranty inspections. See [new construction inspections](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/new-construction-inspection/).
- **Condo:** focuses on the unit and asks what the HOA maintains. Roofs and structure are often common elements.
- **Manufactured home:** adds the HUD data plate, tie-downs, piers, skirting and the marriage line. See [mobile home inspections](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/mobile-home-inspection/).

## What a home inspection does not cover

A standard inspection is visual and non-invasive. Inspectors do not open walls, test for radon or mold, scope the sewer line, or check pools, septic systems or wells unless you book those services. If the home is older, wooded or on a well and septic, book the add-ons early: [radon testing](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/radon-testing/), [sewer scope](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/sewer-scope-inspection/), [termite inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/termite-inspection/), [septic inspection](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/septic-inspection/).

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### What is on a home inspection checklist?
The roof, exterior, foundation and crawlspace, attic and insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling, interior rooms, fireplace and garage. Inspectors follow a standard of practice that lists what they must inspect and report in each area.
:::

::: faq
### Can I use this checklist instead of hiring an inspector?
No. It helps you follow along and ask good questions. A trained inspector has the tools, access and experience to find defects you would miss, and writes a report you can use in negotiations.
:::

::: faq
### Is there a printable home inspection checklist PDF?
Yes. Use the Print / save PDF button in the tool and choose Save as PDF in your browser's print dialog.
:::

::: faq
### Is my checklist saved?
Your ticks, flags and notes are saved in this browser only. Nothing is sent to us. Clearing your browser data removes them.
:::

::: faq
### What should sellers check before a home inspection?
Make sure utilities are on, clear access to the attic, panel, water heater and crawlspace, replace the furnace filter, test smoke and CO alarms, and gather repair receipts and permits.
:::

::: faq
### What does a new construction checklist add?
Phase inspections: before the foundation pour, before drywall, before the final walkthrough and before the 11-month builder warranty ends.
:::
