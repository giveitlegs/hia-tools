# Home Inspector License Requirements by State

36 of 51 jurisdictions (50 states plus DC) require a state home inspector license. 12 have no statewide license, and 3 (Iowa, Nebraska, Pennsylvania) regulate inspectors in a narrower way. Pick a state below to see training hours, the exam, insurance minimums and continuing education, each checked against the state regulator on 2026-11-21.

## How to use this table

1. Choose your state in the tool, or search the table.
2. Read the training hours and exam first. Those decide how long licensing takes.
3. Check the insurance column before you price your services. Several states require general liability or errors and omissions (E&O) coverage before they issue a license.
4. Open the regulator link and confirm the current rules before you pay for a course. Fees and forms change more often than statutes.

## States with no home inspector license

[California](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-california/), [Colorado](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-colorado/), [District of Columbia](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-washington-dc/), [Georgia](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-georgia/), [Hawaii](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-hawaii/), [Idaho](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-idaho/), [Kansas](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-kansas/), [Maine](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-maine/), [Michigan](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-michigan/), [Minnesota](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-minnesota/), [Missouri](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-missouri/), [Wyoming](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector-wyoming/) have no statewide license as of 2026-11-21. In these states, anyone can sell home inspections. Buyers there should ask about training, association membership, insurance and sample reports before they book. Some cities and counties add their own rules, and California sets consumer rules for inspectors in its Business and Professions Code (sections 7195 to 7199).

## Recent changes

- **Utah** created a Private Home Inspector license, enforced since May 1, 2026, with $500,000 each of liability and E&O insurance.
- **Montana** moved from registration to a full license on January 1, 2026.
- **Iowa** limited report-writing to members of qualifying associations from July 1, 2025.
- **Georgia** and **Missouri** saw licensing bills in 2026. We list both as unlicensed until a law takes effect.

## Methodology

We built one record per jurisdiction from the state regulator's website, the licensing statute or the administrative code. National association summaries from [our career guides](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/how-to-become-a-home-inspector/), ASHI and InterNACHI were used only to cross-check. Where an official page did not state a figure, the cell shows a dash rather than a guess. The full dataset, with a source URL for each state, is public on [GitHub](https://github.com/giveitlegs/hia-tools/blob/main/data/licensing.json).

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### Do you need a license to be a home inspector?
In 36 of 51 jurisdictions, yes. 12 states, including California, Colorado and Michigan, have no statewide license, and 3 states regulate inspectors in a narrower way. Check the table above for your state.
:::

::: faq
### How many hours of training does a home inspector license take?
It ranges widely. Among states that set a number, requirements run from about 35 hours (Virginia) to 194 hours (Texas) and 200 hours (North Carolina). Some states add supervised field inspections on top of classroom hours.
:::

::: faq
### What exam do home inspectors take?
Most licensing states use the National Home Inspector Examination (NHIE). Texas, North Carolina and a few others use their own state exam, and some states add a state law or ethics exam.
:::

::: faq
### Do home inspectors need insurance?
Many licensing states require general liability, errors and omissions (E&O) coverage, or a bond. Minimums range from about $50,000 to $500,000. See the insurance column above and our home inspector insurance estimator.
:::

::: faq
### Which states do not license home inspectors?
As of 2026-11-21: California, Colorado, District of Columbia, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Wyoming.
:::

::: faq
### Can I use my license in another state?
Usually not directly. A few states accept the NHIE or another state's license as part of the application, but each state issues its own license. Contact the regulator in the new state before you schedule work there.
:::

::: faq
### How often do home inspectors renew their license?
Most states renew every one or two years and require continuing education each cycle, commonly 8 to 40 hours. New Mexico uses a three-year license.
:::
