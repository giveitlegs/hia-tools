# 4-Point & Wind Mitigation Savings Estimator

In Florida, a **wind mitigation inspection** documents features such as a hip roof, strong roof-to-wall straps and impact-rated windows on form OIR-B1-1802, and insurers must offer discounts for them. A **4-point inspection** (roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC) is usually required to insure older homes: Citizens asks for one on homes over 20 years old. Answer a few questions below to see which features may earn credits and whether your insurer is likely to ask for a 4-point.

## How to use the estimator

1. Enter the year the home was built and the year the roof was last fully replaced.
2. Pick the answers that match the form: roof covering, deck attachment, roof-to-wall connection, roof shape, secondary water resistance and opening protection. "Unknown" is fine. An inspector can confirm.
3. Read the credit potential and open "Feature by feature" for the notes on each answer.
4. Check the 4-point and roof-age guidance before you shop for insurance.

The estimator gives a **qualitative** band (minimal, limited, moderate, strong). Each insurer files its own discount factors with the Office of Insurance Regulation, so we do not promise a dollar amount.

## The wind mitigation form (OIR-B1-1802 Rev. 04/26)

The current form has 9 questions. It has been required for inspections completed on or after 2026-04-01.

| # | Question | What the answers usually mean for credits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Building Code | A: Homes permitted under the FBC (2002 and later) usually get building-code credits; B: Same as A: code-built homes usually get FBC-based credits; C: Treated as code-equivalent for Miami-Dade and Broward homes; D: No building-code discount |
| 2 | Region | HVHZ: New on Rev; R1: Classification only; not a credit by itself.; R2: Classification only; not a credit by itself.; R3: Classification only; not a credit by itself. |
| 3 | Roof Slope | GE_6_12: New on Rev; LT_6_12: No credit impact at Citizens. |
| 4 | Roof Covering | A: Usually earns the roof-cover credit; B: Usually earns the roof-cover credit; C: No roof-cover credit; D: No roof-cover credit (Non-FBC Equivalent at Citizens). |
| 5 | Roof Deck Attachment | A: Weakest rated level (at least 55 psf); B: Middle level (at least 103 psf); C: Strongest wood-deck level (at least 182 psf); D: Treated as a top-tier deck |
| 6 | Roof to Wall Attachment | A: Weakest connection; B: Usually earns a roof-to-wall credit on older homes; C: Usually a larger credit than clips; D: Strongest strap connection |
| 7 | Roof Geometry | A: Usually one of the larger, easier credits; B: Applies only to buildings with 5 or more units; C: No geometry credit |

The form is valid for up to 5 years if the structure has not changed.

## Who can sign a wind mitigation form

- Home inspector licensed under s. 468.8314, F.S., who has completed the statutory number of hours of hurricane mitigation training approved by the Construction Industry Licensing Board and completion of a proficiency exam (statute: at least 3 hours)
- Building code inspector certified under s. 468.607, F.S.
- General, building, or residential contractor licensed under s. 489.111, F.S.
- Professional engineer licensed under s. 471.015, F.S.
- Professional architect licensed under s. 481.213, F.S.
- Any other individual or entity recognized by the insurer as possessing the necessary qualifications to properly complete a uniform mitigation verification form

## 4-point inspections

- **Covers:** roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC.
- **Citizens:** Required for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications on homes more than 20 years old. Form: 4-Point Inspection Form (Insp4pt 03 25).
- **Private carriers:** most ask at 20 or more years, some at 25 to 30. Report validity runs from 30 days to 1 year depending on the carrier. The Florida CFO guide puts the cost at about $100–$300.
- **Citizens roof rule:** Roofs older than 25 years (soft roofs such as shingle) or 50 years (hard roofs such as tile, slate, clay, concrete or metal) require documentation showing at least five years of remaining useful life.
- **Citizens roof rule:** If under five years remain, proof of a full-roof replacement is required before any policy is written.

## Florida roof-age law

- An insurer may not refuse to issue or refuse to renew a homeowner's policy insuring a residential structure with a roof that is less than 15 years old solely because of the age of the roof. (s. 627.7011(5)(b), F.S.)
- For a roof that is at least 15 years old, an insurer must allow a homeowner to have a roof inspection performed by an authorized inspector at the homeowner's expense before requiring the replacement of the roof as a condition of issuing or renewing a homeowner's insurance policy. (s. 627.7011(5)(c), F.S.)
- The insurer may not refuse to issue or refuse to renew a homeowner's insurance policy solely because of roof age if an inspection of the roof performed by an authorized inspector indicates that the roof has 5 years or more of useful life remaining. (s. 627.7011(5)(c), F.S.)

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### How much can a wind mitigation inspection save?
It depends on your home and insurer. Florida law requires insurers to offer discounts for wind-resistant features, and opening protection, a hip roof and strong roof-to-wall connections usually earn the largest credits. Ask your insurer for its mitigation discount table.
:::

::: faq
### How long is a wind mitigation report good for?
Up to 5 years, as long as no material changes are made to the structure and the form has no inaccuracies.
:::

::: faq
### What is the difference between a 4-point and a wind mitigation inspection?
A 4-point checks the condition of the roof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC so an insurer can decide whether to cover an older home. A wind mitigation inspection documents features that reduce wind damage so you can get discounts.
:::

::: faq
### When is a 4-point inspection required in Florida?
Citizens requires one for homes more than 20 years old. Private carriers usually set the threshold at 20 to 30 years.
:::

::: faq
### Can an insurer refuse my policy because of roof age?
Not solely because of age if the roof is under 15 years old. For older roofs, you can get a roof inspection, and an insurer may not refuse solely due to age if the roof has at least 5 years of useful life left (s. 627.7011, F.S.).
:::

::: faq
### Who can do a wind mitigation inspection?
A Florida licensed home inspector with hurricane mitigation training, a certified building code inspector, a licensed general, building or residential contractor, a professional engineer, an architect, or another person your insurer accepts.
:::

::: faq
### How much does a 4-point inspection cost?
The Florida CFO consumer guide puts a standalone 4-point at about $100 to $300. Many inspectors bundle it with a wind mitigation inspection or a full home inspection.
:::
