# Home Inspection Report Form Template

A free home inspection form you can use two ways: print a blank report form and fill it in by hand on site, or fill it in on screen and rate every item as inspected, not inspected, not present or deficient. Pick the sections you need, add your own items, then print it, save it as a PDF, or download a Word or spreadsheet copy. Everything stays in your browser.

## How to use the report form

1. Choose **Blank form to print** for a paper worksheet, or **Fill in on screen** to complete the report here.
2. Enter your company, license number, client, property address, date, weather and who attended.
3. Tick the sections to include. Laundry, pool and spa, and ancillary services are off by default.
4. In fill-in mode, open each section, rate each item and type a comment with the location and your recommendation. Add items that are specific to the house.
5. Print or save a PDF, or download a Word (.doc) or spreadsheet (.csv) copy. Deficient items are pulled into a summary at the top.

## What the form covers

The sections follow the systems named in the InterNACHI and ASHI standards of practice:

- **Roof:** covering, flashing, penetrations, gutters and how you inspected it.
- **Exterior:** cladding, doors, windows, decks, railings, grading and drainage.
- **Structure and foundation:** foundation, basement or crawlspace, floor, wall and roof structure, moisture.
- **Attic, insulation and ventilation:** access, insulation depth, ventilation and exhaust fan terminations.
- **Electrical:** service, main panel, subpanels, breakers, wiring type, grounding, GFCI and AFCI protection, alarms.
- **Plumbing and water heater:** supply and drain piping, fixtures, shut-offs, fuel, TPR valve, venting.
- **Heating and cooling:** equipment, operation, distribution, filters, flues and condensate.
- **Fireplaces, interior, kitchen, bathrooms and garage:** including the garage fire separation and opener auto-reverse.
- **Optional:** laundry, pool and spa, and ancillary services such as radon, sewer scope and WDO.

## Rating key

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| IN | Inspected and functioning as intended at the time of inspection |
| NI | Not inspected; give the reason in the comments |
| NP | Component not present |
| D | Deficient; describe the defect and recommend an action |

Each rating needs a comment when it is NI or D. Say where the item is, what you saw, why it matters and who should evaluate or repair it. The [sample home inspection report](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/pages/sample-home-inspection-report/) shows how a finished narrative reads.

## Paper form or report software?

A printable form works well for new inspectors, ride-alongs, practice inspections and as a field worksheet behind your software. Most working inspectors deliver the final report through [home inspection software](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/pages/home-inspection-software/), which adds photos, templates and online delivery. Many states also set minimum report content, so check your [state's license requirements](https://homeinspectoratlas.com/pages/home-inspector-license-requirements/) before you use any form for paid work.

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
### Is this home inspection form free?
Yes. The form, the Word download and the spreadsheet download are free, with no sign-up. Your entries are saved only in this browser.
:::

::: faq
### Can I download the form as a PDF or Word file?
Yes. Use Print / save PDF and choose Save as PDF in the print dialog, or download a Word (.doc) file that opens in Microsoft Word and Google Docs. A .csv download opens in Excel or Google Sheets.
:::

::: faq
### What should a home inspection report include?
At minimum: the client, property and date, the scope and limitations, each system inspected, what was not inspected and why, and every deficiency with its location and a recommendation. Many states set their own minimum report content.
:::

::: faq
### What do IN, NI, NP and D mean on an inspection form?
IN means inspected, NI means not inspected, NP means not present and D means deficient. Texas uses the same four categories (I, NI, NP, D) on its TREC property inspection report, and many software templates follow the same pattern.
:::

::: faq
### Can I use this form instead of inspection software?
For practice, ride-alongs or a simple paper worksheet, yes. Most working inspectors use software for photos, report delivery and client agreements, and use a paper form as a backup.
:::

::: faq
### Does this form meet my state's requirements?
It follows the InterNACHI and ASHI standards of practice, but some states mandate a specific report form or content. Texas, for example, requires inspectors to use the TREC property inspection report form. Check your state's rules before using it for paid inspections.
:::
